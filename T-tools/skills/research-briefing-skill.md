# Research Briefing Skill — StoreNext

Turn research into a scannable brief that informs content strategy and sales positioning.

## Standard Briefing Structure

### 1. Executive Summary (2-3 sentences)
Key finding in plain language. What does this mean for StoreNext?

### 2. Key Findings (3-5 bullets)
Each = one sentence with a specific number or detail.

### 3. Analysis (1-2 paragraphs)
What this means for StoreNext's positioning, content, or pipeline strategy.

### 4. Recommendations (2-3 actions)
Concrete. Tied to findings. Not generic.

### 5. Sources & Limitations

---

## Templates

### Competitor Analysis Brief

```
Executive Summary:
[Competitor X] positions as [X], targets [audience], owns [content angle].
StoreNext's advantage: [specific differentiator].
Opportunity: [gap in their coverage we can exploit].

Key Findings:
1. Positioning: "[Their exact claim]" — targets [company size/type]
2. Content angle: [What they post about on LinkedIn]
3. Pricing: [If available] OR [Based on team size/customer list: est. $X]
4. Weakness: [What G2 reviews or customers say they're missing]
5. Recent move: [Product launch, partnership, or content shift in last 90 days]

Analysis:
[What their positioning leaves open for StoreNext to own]
[What we should NOT compete on directly]

Recommendations:
1. Own [specific angle] — they're not covering it
2. Differentiate on [specific proof point]: 300+ clients, 2M transactions/day, 20B₪ processed
3. Watch for: [competitive move to monitor]

Sources: [URLs + dates]
```

### Market Trend Brief

```
Executive Summary:
[Trend] is [growing/shifting] at [rate]. For CFOs and Procurement Leaders, this means [specific impact]. StoreNext is positioned to [benefit/address this].

Key Findings:
1. Market size/growth: [data + source]
2. Procurement tech adoption: [% or trend]
3. CFO priority shift: [what finance leaders are now focused on]
4. Supply chain risk: [McKinsey/Gartner stat]

Analysis:
[Where this trend is heading in 6-12 months]
[How it maps to StoreNext's three platforms: Core, Meteor, Data & Analytics]

Recommendations:
1. [Content angle to publish now — trend-riding opportunity]
2. [Sales angle: how to reference this in conversations]
3. [What to watch for]

Sources: [URLs + dates]
```

### Content Strategy Brief

```
Executive Summary:
Topic: [X]. Audience: [CFO / Procurement / CIO]. Recommended angle: [X].

Key Findings:
1. Pain point evidence: [Stat or signal showing this is real]
2. Current coverage: [What already exists — what we can differentiate from]
3. Engagement signal: [Any LinkedIn data on this topic performing well]
4. Product fit: [How StoreNext maps to this topic]

Recommended Structure:
[Hook], [Problem framing], [McKinsey/Gartner stat], [Solution frame], [CTA]

Sources: [URLs + dates]
```

## Writing Rules

- **Lead with the conclusion** — first sentence = the finding, not the setup
- **Numbers over adjectives** — "34% YoY growth" not "significant growth"
- **Label certainty:**
  - Fact: "Per McKinsey 2026..."
  - Estimated: "Based on headcount and pricing, est. $5-10M ARR"
  - Signal: "3 LinkedIn posts in 30 days suggest they're expanding focus to..."
- **CFO standard:** If you wouldn't say it to a CFO, don't write it

---

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] Executive summary = standalone (full picture in 2-3 sentences)?
- [ ] At least 3 numbers from credible sources?
- [ ] Facts vs opinions clearly labeled?
- [ ] StoreNext implications explicit (not implied)?
- [ ] Recommendations are specific actions?
- [ ] Sources listed with dates?

---

*A briefing that doesn't change what we do next isn't a briefing. It's a report.*
