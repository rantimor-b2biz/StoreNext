# Case Study Skill — StoreNext

Turn client results into the most powerful sales asset in B2B: proof.

## When to Use

- A client has achieved measurable results with StoreNext
- Sales team needs proof for a specific objection or use case
- Content team needs social proof for LinkedIn or blog
- Website needs a new case study page

---

## Case Study Types

| Format | Use | Length |
|--------|-----|--------|
| **Full Case Study** | Website, PDF, deep sales tool | 600-900 words |
| **Mini Case Study** | LinkedIn post, email snippet | 150-250 words |
| **Stat Callout** | Embedded in blog posts, presentations | 1-2 sentences |
| **Customer Quote Card** | Social media, sales decks | 1 quote + context |

---

## Full Case Study Structure

```
## [Company Name or Anonymized Type]: [Headline Result in Numbers]

### The Challenge
[2-3 sentences describing the operational problem BEFORE StoreNext]
- Who the company is (size, industry, supplier complexity)
- What they were struggling with (specific — not generic)
- What it was costing them (time, money, risk)

### Why StoreNext
[1-2 sentences: Why they chose StoreNext over alternatives]
Not: "They chose our amazing platform."
But: "They needed direct SAP integration and multi-language supplier communication."

### The Implementation
[2-3 sentences: What was deployed, timeline]
- Which StoreNext platform(s): Core / Meteor / Data & Analytics
- Timeline to go live
- Any key integration point

### The Results
[4-6 specific, quantified outcomes]
- [Metric]: [X%] reduction in [specific thing]
- [Metric]: [X hours/week] saved on [specific task]
- [Metric]: [X days] reduction in [DSO / approval cycle / supplier response time]
- [Metric]: [X suppliers] onboarded in [Y time]

### Customer Quote
"[Direct quote from CFO, Procurement Leader, or CIO]"
— [Name or title], [Company or industry]

### Key Takeaway
[One sentence: The core lesson for other enterprise organizations]
```

## Mini Case Study (LinkedIn / Email)

```
[Company type, anonymized]: [Industry, size]
Challenge: [The problem in one sentence]
Result: [3 specific numbers]
Quote: "[Customer voice]"

This is what supplier portal infrastructure looks like in practice.
```

---

## Writing Rules

### Rule 1: Numbers or It Didn't Happen
Every case study needs at least 3 quantified results. "Improved efficiency" is not a result.

```
Not: "Significantly reduced manual work"
Yes: "Reduced supplier inquiry time from 4 days average to same-day"
```

### Rule 2: Customer's Words, Not Marketing's
The best case studies sound like a peer talking to a peer:
```
Not: "StoreNext's revolutionary platform transformed their procurement"
Yes: "We finally stopped managing suppliers through email chains"
```

### Rule 3: The Problem First
Start with the challenge, not the solution. Buyers identify with problems before they trust solutions.

### Rule 4: Infrastructure Language
Position StoreNext as infrastructure the organization depends on — not a tool they use occasionally.

### Rule 5: StoreNext Voice Throughout
- Specific metrics only
- Enterprise language (no startup-style enthusiasm)
- Transparent about implementation (doesn't oversimplify)
- No hype words

---

## What to Avoid

- Vague results ("improved significantly")
- Marketing speak ("game-changing", "transformational")
- Feature lists without outcomes
- Generic challenges that apply to any company
- Em dashes (—)

---

## Quality Checklist

- [ ] At least 3 quantified results with specific numbers?
- [ ] Customer voice included (quote)?
- [ ] Challenge section specific enough to be recognizable?
- [ ] No hype language?
- [ ] Infrastructure positioning (not feature list)?
- [ ] No em dashes?
- [ ] CFO would find this credible?
