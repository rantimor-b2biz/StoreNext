# LinkedIn Post Writer Skill — StoreNext

Three writing styles for CFO, Procurement Director, and CIO audiences. Each style has a distinct goal, structure, and rhythm. Pick the style first, then write.

**Recommended weekly rhythm:** 2x HubSpot, 1x Salesforce, 2x Canva

---

## STYLE 1: HubSpot — Education + Lead Gen

**Goal:** Teach something practical. Make the reader feel smarter. Position StoreNext as the expert.

**When to use:** Pain-point education, process explainers, "why it happens" posts, 3-step frameworks for CFOs and Procurement Directors.

**Structure:**
```
HOOK (1-2 lines) — Clear pain or surprising insight
Insight/frame (2-4 lines) — Why this happens, what most orgs get wrong
Steps or proof (3-5 bullets) — Concrete, numbered, actionable
CTA (1 line) — Open question that invites a real answer
```

**Hook formulas:**
- "Stop managing supplier invoices by email. Start managing them by data."
- "The problem isn't your ERP. It's the gap between your ERP and your suppliers."
- "Most enterprises don't have a procurement problem. They have a visibility problem."
- "65% of P2P exceptions come from the same 3 process gaps. Most CFOs don't know which ones."

**Sentence rules:**
- Max 15 words per sentence
- Use ✓ for checkmarks and → for flow (not bullet points)
- Short lines. One idea per line.

**StoreNext-specific examples:**

Pain: Invoice approval cycle taking 12+ days
```
Supplier invoices shouldn't sit in someone's inbox for two weeks.

Most do. Here's why:

→ No central workflow connecting PO to invoice to approval
→ ERP shows the commitment but not the status
→ Finance team chasing approvers manually

The fix isn't urgency. It's structure.

Three things that close the approval gap:
✓ Automatic three-way matching at submission
✓ Exception routing by rule, not by chase
✓ Full audit trail visible to both sides

How long does your current approval cycle take?
```

## STYLE 2: Salesforce — Thought Leadership, C-Level

**Goal:** Position StoreNext as the strategic partner for enterprise transformation. Speak to the CFO who sets the agenda, not just solves today's problem.

**When to use:** Industry trends, strategic shifts, procurement transformation angles, posts aimed at CFOs and CIOs.

**Structure:**
```
HOOK (1-2 lines) — Big trend or strategic observation
Strategic insight (2-4 lines) — What this means for enterprise finance/procurement
3 shifts happening now — Use → not bullets
Debate question (1 line) — Invites disagreement or perspective
```

**Hook formulas:**
- "The enterprises winning in 2026 aren't those with the most suppliers — they're the ones with the most supplier visibility."
- "Finance automation isn't a technology decision. It's an organizational maturity decision."
- "The CFOs outperforming peers in 2026 aren't cutting costs. They're automating the work that costs them."
- "ERP systems were built to record transactions. They weren't built to prevent them from going wrong."

**Formatting rule:** Use → instead of bullets. No em dashes. Periods end every thought.

**StoreNext-specific example:**

```
The finance teams closing faster in 2026 aren't working harder.

They're working with better infrastructure.

Three shifts separating them from the rest:

→ From email-based supplier communication to portal-based status visibility
→ From manual reconciliation to automated three-way matching
→ From reactive exception handling to proactive exception prevention

The gap isn't technology. It's the decision to stop tolerating manual processes.

Where is your organization on this shift?
```

## STYLE 3: Canva — Fast Engagement, Virality

**Goal:** Maximum scroll-stop. Short, punchy, easy to share. Built for reach, not depth.

**When to use:** Relatable operational truths, stat-based posts, before/after comparisons, high-frequency content.

**Structure:**
```
HOOK (1-2 very short lines)
Body (3-5 items, 5-10 words each)
CTA — "Comment X and I'll DM it" or a direct question
```

**Sentence rules:**
- 5-10 words per line maximum
- Break after every thought
- Very short. Very fast.

**StoreNext-specific examples:**

```
Enterprise procurement in 2026:

Still approving invoices by email.
Still chasing suppliers by phone.
Still reconciling in spreadsheets.

The infrastructure exists to fix all three.

Which one costs your team the most hours?
```

```
Signs your P2P process needs an upgrade:

→ Finance team knows suppliers by first name (from all the calls)
→ Month-end close takes 8+ days
→ No one can see commitment vs. actuals in real time
→ Your ERP and your suppliers don't talk to each other

How many apply to your organization?
```

## Input Format — How to Request a Post

```
Write a LinkedIn post in [HubSpot / Salesforce / Canva] style about:
- Topic: [e.g. supplier invoice approval cycle]
- Audience: [CFO / Procurement Director / CIO]
- Pain point: [e.g. invoices stuck in email approval chains]
- Key insight: [e.g. 67% of P2P delays trace to missing PO-invoice matching]
- Action: [e.g. 3 steps to automate invoice routing]
- CTA type: [open question / debate invitation / minimal commitment]
```

---

## Pre-Publish Checklist

- [ ] Hook is 1-2 lines — stops scroll before truncation?
- [ ] Clear pain point stated?
- [ ] 3-5 bullets or steps max?
- [ ] CTA is specific and easy to answer?
- [ ] Sentences under 15 words?
- [ ] New insight — not generic advice?
- [ ] Relevant to CFO, Procurement Director, or CIO?
- [ ] Zero em dashes (—)?
- [ ] Zero hype words (revolutionary, game-changing, transformative)?
- [ ] No external link in caption?

---

## StoreNext Voice Reminders

- Speak peer-to-peer. You are a CFO/Procurement peer, not a vendor.
- Data anchors credibility. One specific number beats three vague claims.
- "Easy" is forbidden. Enterprise challenges are not simple.
- Formal but not stuffy. 8/10 formality.
- Company events and culture posts: end on a strong statement, NOT a discussion question.
