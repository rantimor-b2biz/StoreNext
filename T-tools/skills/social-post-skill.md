# Social Post Skill — StoreNext LinkedIn

Write LinkedIn posts that enterprise buyers stop to read. No hype. Data, insight, peer voice.

## Client Context

**Platform:** LinkedIn (primary — only B2B social channel)
**Persona:** CFO, Procurement Leader, CIO
**Voice:** Enterprise professional. Peer-to-peer. Data-driven. Measured.
**Goal:** Build authority, generate pipeline conversations, demonstrate product relevance
**See also:** `C-core/voice-dna.md`, `M-memory/learning-log.md` (gold standard post)

---

## Post Length

- **Standard:** 150-300 words
- **Event-driven (crisis/timely):** Up to 350 words with bullets
- **Never:** Longer than 400 words without a very strong reason

---

## Hook Formulas for Enterprise B2B

### Hook Type 1: The Visibility Gap
```
Your suppliers know about [disruption] before you do.
Your organization may find out days later.
```
Works because: Creates urgency without drama. The CFO immediately feels the operational gap.

### Hook Type 2: The Operational Reality
```
Most enterprises spend [X hours/week] doing [manual task].
That's [Y FTEs] dedicated to something that should be automatic.
```
Works because: Specific. Quantified. CFO-legible.

### Hook Type 3: The Question Nobody Asks
```
When was the last time you knew — in real time — whether all your suppliers were operating normally?
```
Works because: Exposes the gap without accusation.

### Hook Type 4: The Stat + So What
```
According to McKinsey, companies face supply chain disruptions lasting a month or more every 3.7 years.
Most organizations only discover disruptions after they've already been impacted.
```
Works because: Credibility source + real operational consequence.

## StoreNext Voice Rules

**DO:**
- Use specific metrics ("80% reduction in supplier calls", "20B₪ processed")
- Lead with the problem (not the product)
- Speak peer-to-peer (not vendor-to-buyer)
- Use McKinsey/Gartner as credibility anchors
- End with a discussion question that CFOs/procurement leaders would answer

**DON'T:**
- Use hype: "revolutionary", "game-changing", "unprecedented"
- Say "easy" — enterprise challenges aren't simple
- Start with company name or product features
- External links in caption (kills reach — put in first comment)
- Em dashes (—) — permanent standard
