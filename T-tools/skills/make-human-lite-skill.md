# Make Human Lite — StoreNext Edition
*A skill for making AI-generated text sound actually human*

---

## Client Context

**Client:** StoreNext — Enterprise B2B, CFO and Procurement audience
**Voice:** Professional, peer-to-peer, data-driven — like a senior procurement executive talking to a peer
**Key risk:** Enterprise AI text sounds like a vendor brochure. CFOs stop reading vendor brochures.

**The StoreNext test:** Would a CFO who gets 50 vendor emails a week stop to read this — or delete it?

---

## The Problem

AI text sounds like AI. Too polished. Too balanced. Too... complete.

CFOs read it and think: "This is marketing."

And then they move on.

---

## The Solution

Rewrite so it sounds like a peer sharing an observation, not a vendor making a pitch.

The goal: **credibility + recognition**.

---

## The Core Idea: Peer Observation, Not Vendor Narration

AI writes like a vendor brochure. Complete. Polished. Promising everything.

A CFO peer writes like they're sharing something they noticed. Specific. Direct. With their own skepticism built in.

**AI (Vendor Narration):**
> "Our comprehensive supplier portal solution enables enterprises to achieve real-time visibility across their entire vendor ecosystem."

**Human (Peer Observation):**
> "Most procurement teams I talk to can't answer a simple question in real time: are all our suppliers operating normally right now? They have to check three systems and send emails."

Feel the difference?

The human version has:
- A specific, observable reality
- The mess acknowledged ("three systems and send emails")
- No product mention — just the problem recognized

## The Human Touches

### 1. Add the Operational Mess

AI describes clean processes. Real enterprises are messier.

| AI | Human |
|---|---|
| "Centralized supplier communication" | "Instead of sending 40 emails to find out what's happening, one message goes to everyone" |
| "Real-time visibility into your supplier base" | "You can actually see what's happening — not discover it three days after it started" |
| "Streamlined procurement workflows" | "Less spreadsheet archaeology, more actual decisions" |

### 2. Use the CFO's Language

AI uses vendor language. CFOs use operational language.

**Swap:**
- "leverage our platform" → "use it"
- "achieve visibility" → "know what's happening"
- "drive ROI" → "actually save money" or "justify the cost"
- "enable digital transformation" → "stop running on spreadsheets"
- "solutions" → "tools" or just name the thing
- "ecosystem" → "suppliers" or "vendors"

### 3. Include the Doubt

AI-written enterprise content is always certain. Real practitioners aren't.

| AI (Certain) | Human (Real) |
|---|---|
| "Enterprises that implement supplier portals see 30% cost reduction" | "The numbers vary. But most teams tell us the bigger gain is not the cost — it's knowing what's happening before it becomes a crisis." |
| "This approach is proven to work" | "We've seen this work. It doesn't fix everything, but the visibility piece alone changes how procurement reacts to disruption." |

### 4. Make the Problem Vivid, Not Abstract

AI states problems abstractly. Humans describe the moment the problem hits.

**AI:** "Enterprises lack real-time supplier visibility."

**Human:** "A disruption starts. Your suppliers know about it immediately. Your procurement team finds out days later — usually from a delay notification, not from a dashboard."

## Quick Fixes — StoreNext Specific

When you spot these enterprise AI patterns, fix them immediately:

| If you see this... | Change it to... |
|---|---|
| "In today's complex supply chain environment..." | (Delete. Start with the problem.) |
| "Enterprises are increasingly faced with..." | (Delete. State what they're actually dealing with.) |
| "Achieve end-to-end visibility" | "Know what's happening across your suppliers" |
| "Streamline your procurement processes" | "Make procurement less manual" |
| "Leverage our platform to..." | "Use [the portal] to..." |
| "Drive operational excellence" | (Find what this actually means and say that.) |
| "Best-in-class solution" | (Delete. Describe the actual capability.) |
| "Holistic approach to supplier management" | (Delete. Name the specific thing.) |
| "Empower your procurement team" | "Give your team the information they need" |
| "In conclusion, it is clear that..." | (Delete. End on the point, not a summary.) |

---

## Hard Rules — Universal (Apply to All Content)

### Never Use:
- **Em dashes (—)** — the #1 AI tell. Use periods, commas, or colons instead.
- Corporate superlatives: best-in-class, world-class, industry-leading, cutting-edge
- Hype verbs: revolutionize, transform, disrupt, unlock, supercharge
- Filler openers: "In today's...", "In an era of...", "More than ever..."
- Empty closers: "In conclusion...", "At the end of the day..."

### Always Include:
- The specific operational reality (not the abstract problem)
- At least one moment where the complexity or messiness is acknowledged
- A peer-to-peer register — CFO talking to CFO, not vendor talking to buyer

## Before/After — StoreNext Examples

### Example 1: LinkedIn post hook

**Before (AI):**
```
In today's dynamic supply chain environment, enterprises face unprecedented challenges
in maintaining real-time visibility across their supplier networks. A robust supplier
portal solution can help procurement teams achieve the visibility they need.
```

**After (Human):**
```
A disruption starts. Your suppliers know about it immediately.
Your procurement team finds out days later.

That gap is not a technology problem. It's a visibility problem.
And most enterprises are still solving it with email.
```

---

### Example 2: Problem description

**Before (AI):**
```
Enterprises struggle with supplier communication inefficiencies that lead to
procurement delays and increased operational risk.
```

**After (Human):**
```
When a disruption hits, most procurement teams spend the first 48 hours just trying
to understand what is happening. Emails go out. Responses trickle in. Someone
updates a spreadsheet. Decisions wait.

That is the real cost — not the disruption itself, but the response time.
```

---

## The Final Test (StoreNext)

Before publishing, ask:

1. Would a CFO read this — or recognize it as vendor content and move on?
2. Is the problem described in operational terms, not abstract terms?
3. Is there at least one moment of acknowledged complexity or uncertainty?
4. Are there any em dashes? (Delete them.)
5. Does it sound like a peer observation, not a marketing claim?

---

## Output Rules

When using this skill:
- Return only the rewritten text
- No commentary or explanations
- No before/after comparisons
