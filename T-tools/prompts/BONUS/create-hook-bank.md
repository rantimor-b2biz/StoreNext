# Create Hook Bank — StoreNext

Build a reference bank of proven LinkedIn hooks for CFO and Procurement Director audiences.

## When to Use

- Once, to build the initial hook bank
- Quarterly, to add new patterns from top-performing posts
- When a LinkedIn post feels flat in the opening line
- When content drafts keep starting with "In today's competitive landscape..."

---

## The 4 Enterprise Hook Formulas (StoreNext-Specific)

These are the approved hooks from the gold standard post (M-memory/learning-log.md). Every new hook should follow one of these patterns.

### Formula 1: The Visibility Gap
**Pattern:** "Your [stakeholder] knows X. Your [organization] discovers it Y later."
**Logic:** Creates tension between what suppliers/vendors know and what procurement does not.

**Examples:**
- "A disruption starts. Your suppliers know immediately. Your procurement team finds out days later."
- "Your top vendor made a major change. Your organization learned about it three weeks after the fact."
- "Compliance risk entered your supply chain in Q3. It showed up in your audit in Q1."

---

### Formula 2: The Operational Reality
**Pattern:** Describe the messy internal reality when disruption hits, before introducing a solution.
**Logic:** CFOs recognize the scenario. They do not need to be sold — they need to see that you understand the problem.

**Examples:**
- "When a disruption begins, procurement does not manage the situation. It spends the first 48 hours just trying to understand what is happening."
- "Most enterprise procurement teams know how to respond to a crisis. They do not know how to see it coming."
- "The problem is not the disruption. It is that the answer to 'which suppliers are affected?' takes three days to compile."

---

### Formula 3: The Question Nobody Asks
**Pattern:** Ask the operational question that reveals the gap — not a rhetorical question, a real one buyers ask internally.
**Logic:** The CFO or Procurement Director has asked this exact question in a meeting. Seeing it written stops the scroll.

**Examples:**
- "Are all your suppliers operating normally right now? Could you answer that in under 10 minutes?"
- "How long does it take your team to contact all affected suppliers when a disruption begins?"
- "When was the last time your entire supplier list was accurate, current, and accessible in one place?"

---

### Formula 4: Stat + So What
**Pattern:** Open with a sourced, credible statistic. Follow immediately with what it means for the enterprise buyer.
**Logic:** CFOs expect data. The stat earns the opening. The "so what" earns the read.

**Examples:**
- "Supply chain disruptions lasting one month or more occur every 3.7 years on average (McKinsey). For most enterprises, that means the question is not if — it is when, and how ready you are."
- "70% of enterprises still manage supplier communication through email and spreadsheets (Gartner). When disruption hits, that is the system they rely on."
- "Vendor consolidation can reduce procurement costs by 10-30% (Hackett Group). Most enterprises know this. Few have a clear path to execute it."

## Rules for StoreNext Hooks

- **No hype or urgency language** — "Revolutionary," "critical," "urgent" are red flags for CFO audiences
- **Formal register** — 8/10 formality. Short sentences. Active voice.
- **No em dashes (—)** — Replace with periods, colons, or commas
- **Stat hooks must have sources** — McKinsey, Gartner, Hackett Group, Forrester preferred
- **Scenarios must be enterprise-scale** — If naming a company size, it should be 500+ employees
- **No first-person "I" in hook** — StoreNext posts are from the brand, not an individual
- **Never open with "In today's..." or "Many companies..."** — These are weak, generic openers
