Most companies treat payment fraud as a security problem.
The data says it's a reconciliation problem.

79% of businesses faced fraud attempts last year, according to the AFP's 2026 Payments Fraud and Control Survey.

Only 22% recovered the funds afterward.

That gap is not about weak detection. It's about timing.

Here is why the problem exists.

Most treasury teams reconcile bank activity on a schedule. Daily. Sometimes twice a day. Rarely continuously.

A payment is authorized. It moves. Hours later, someone checks the books against the bank. By then the money has cleared.

The anomaly gets confirmed, not prevented.

And the window is shrinking. Deloitte reports the cash conversion cycle tightened by about 0.9 days in 2025 as teams compress payment cycles.

Faster payments mean less time to catch bad activity before settlement.

This is exactly where real-time, multi-bank reconciliation becomes critical:

- Live bank connectivity instead of overnight batch files
- Coverage across every bank relationship, not just the primary ones
- Payment intent matched against confirmed activity continuously

The insight for finance leaders is simple. You cannot close a real-time exposure with a once-a-day control.

How many hours pass between a payment leaving your organization and the first check against actual bank activity?
