## What's Working

- **Narrative continuity:** The Week 1 callback ("last week") creates a campaign thread without requiring the reader to have seen Week 1. Even if they didn't, the opening still works: "the most common answer procurement leaders gave us" establishes authority and peer validation.
- **The supplier call quotes:** "Where's my PO?" / "When will I be paid?" - these are exact, word-for-word lines every procurement leader has heard. Creates instant recognition and emotional identification.
- **"Your procurement team didn't sign up to be a call center":** This is the emotional pivot. Names the frustration before introducing the solution. Well placed.
- **Solution section parallel structure:** The "They check... They see... They know..." rhythm is clean and readable. Each line is a concrete, verifiable improvement, not a vague claim.
- **"20,000+ suppliers already work this way":** The strongest proof point in the post. Shifts the solution from theoretical to operational. This is the trust builder.
- **Brand positioning line:** "That's not a feature. That's infrastructure." mirrors Week 1's close. On a 13-week campaign, this repetition is intentional and correct. Brand signatures work through repetition.
- **No em dashes:** Confirmed. Compliant with the permanent writing standard.
- **No hype language:** No "revolutionary," "AI-powered," "game-changing." Voice DNA compliant.
- **CTA:** Forward-looking and specific ("10 extra hours per week"). Invites positive engagement rather than venting.

---

## What Needs Work

None requiring revision before publication.

**Minor note (for future weeks):** The copywriter asked whether "call center" or "switchboard" should be the consistent metaphor. For continuity, "call center" is the stronger choice going forward. It describes a department, implies over-resourcing, and has more Enterprise resonance than "switchboard." Log this for the team.
