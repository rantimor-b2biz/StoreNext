Money now moves at 2 a.m. on a Saturday. Most treasury operations still run on banking hours.

That gap is widening every day.

Treasury was built around a clock. Cutoff times. Overnight batch files. End-of-day reconciliation.

Instant payment rails removed that clock. Counterparties settle continuously, with full finality, outside any banking window.

According to DXC's 2026 treasury trends report, instant payments were the most-used business payment method in the United States in 2025. They led ACH and wire by more than 15 percentage points.

The shift already happened. The architecture has not.

Here is why the gap exists. Batch processing was built to be checked once, then trusted until the next cycle. When settlement goes continuous, that model breaks in three places.

Manual portal logins leave blind windows between checks.
Cash pulled by hand is already stale in the ERP.
Idle liquidity buffers grow to cover the uncertainty.

This is exactly where direct bank connectivity and ERP-native payment initiation become critical.

- Cash positions flow automatically from every bank, not a periodic pull
- Payments initiate and approve inside the ERP, not a separate portal
- Visibility and control run on the same timeline as settlement

A faster dashboard on top of batch files only shows stale data sooner. The problem is connectivity, not visualization.

When your counterparties settle continuously, does your cash position reflect a live feed or yesterday's snapshot?
