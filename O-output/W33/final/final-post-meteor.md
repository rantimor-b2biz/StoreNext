Most CFOs filed the SWIFT ISO 20022 migration under "bank IT project."

That assumption is now a liability.

The next hard deadline lands in November 2026. Fully unstructured postal addresses will no longer clear cross-border payment messages.

According to Kyriba, structured Country and Town Name fields become mandatory for RTGS and cross-border transfers.

Here is the part most teams miss.

According to State Street, corporates are formally out of scope of the mandatory timeline. Their banks are not.

So why does this land on the CFO?

Because the structured data has to come from somewhere. According to Kyriba, enriched payment data, including beneficiary addresses and purpose codes, must originate from corporate payment systems. Banks will not add it on a customer's behalf.

The gap falls back on the ERP. Free-text address fields do not clear validation. Manual re-keying does not scale across thousands of monthly payments.

This is exactly where direct ERP-to-bank payment initiation becomes critical:

- Structured, validated payment data leaves the ERP already compliant
- No manual re-entry where unstructured addresses slip through
- No dependency on the bank to reconstruct missing fields

The Bank of Israel has already migrated its Zahav RTGS system to ISO 20022. The direction is structural, not optional, on both sides of the payment.

The deadline is fixed. The remediation window is not.

Is November 2026 a bank problem or an ERP problem in your organization, and who owns it today?
