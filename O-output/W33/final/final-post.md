In 2025, working capital improved. Most of that gain was borrowed from suppliers.

According to Deloitte, the cash conversion cycle shortened by roughly 0.9 days last year.

But the components tell a sharper story. Days Inventory Outstanding fell. Days Sales Outstanding rose. Days Payable Outstanding did the heavy lifting.

In plain terms, companies improved their numbers mainly by paying suppliers later.

Stretching payment terms is a legitimate lever. The risk is in how the decision gets made.

According to RapidRatings, 66% of organizations now rate the supply environment as high or very-high risk. Yet only 15% fully integrate supplier financial health into payment-terms decisions.

That gap has a cause. Payment history sits in the ERP. Supplier communication sits in email. Risk signals sit in a spreadsheet one analyst maintains.

So the DPO decision is made without a structured view of which suppliers are already under stress.

This is exactly where structured supplier visibility becomes critical:

- Supplier communication and documents connected to the ERP, not scattered across inboxes
- An auditable trail for every terms change, dispute, and status flag
- Visibility that reaches past tier one, where concentration risk hides

A payment-terms decision is now a supply chain risk decision.

Managed with real data, it becomes a segmented call, not a blanket policy.

Are your payment-terms decisions informed by supplier financial signals, or made in the dark?
