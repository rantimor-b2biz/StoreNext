Thousands of invoices that never required validation before will require it in less than six months.

The real challenge is not the new regulation. It is the volume.

From January 1, 2026, invoices above NIS 10,000 need a pre-approved allocation number. From June 1, 2026, the threshold drops to NIS 5,000. Per Sovos, VAT Implementation Order 01/2025 skips the planned NIS 15,000 step and pulls a multi-year timeline into six months.

Here is what that means in practice.

Every invoice above NIS 5,000 becomes a compliance checkpoint. If validation happens too late, payments are delayed. If it does not happen at all, VAT recovery is at risk.

Most organizations already have a process for high-value invoices. Very few have a process for validating every invoice above NIS 5,000.

That is the real change. Not a new rule. A new baseline.

At NIS 5,000, qualifying invoices multiply. Manual validation does not scale with that count. It scales worse.

This is where automated allocation-number validation becomes critical:

- Validation at invoice intake, before payment approval
- Matching against invoice value, vendor, and VAT data
- ERP kept as the system of record
- Audit trail ready for review, without manual reconstruction

Globally, Deloitte reports 50% of CFOs rank digital transformation of finance as their top 2026 priority. This mandate does not create that priority. It moves the deadline forward.

The January threshold is a rehearsal. The June threshold is the real test.
