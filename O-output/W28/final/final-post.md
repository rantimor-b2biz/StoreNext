AI just jumped to the number two CFO priority for 2026.

Most finance teams are not ready for it.

Gartner and Evanta's 2026 CFO Leadership Perspectives research shows AI and automation climbing from fourth place to second in a single year.

But a priority ranking is not a readiness score.

Here is the gap most 2026 budget conversations miss.

AI applied to stale data produces forecasts nobody trusts.

The reason is structural. Most Enterprise finance systems still run batch updates, reconcile with a two-day lag, and connect to only a subset of banking relationships.

European treasurers already understand this. Per the EACT survey cited by Cygnetise, they ranked real-time reporting and liquidity visibility as their top technology focus, ahead of AI itself.

They are sequencing correctly. Visibility first, intelligence second.

This is exactly where real-time connectivity becomes critical. Before AI can produce measurable ROI, the data underneath it has to be current and reconciled.

That means:

- Bank connectivity in real time, not batch downloads
- ERP integration without manual export cycles
- Reconciliation that runs continuously, not at month-end
- Exception rates you can actually measure

The CFOs who move fastest from AI intent to AI ROI will be the ones who fixed connectivity first.

Visibility is the requirement. AI is the capability built on top of it.

Heading into 2026 budget season, which comes first in your plan: the AI use case or the data plumbing beneath it?
