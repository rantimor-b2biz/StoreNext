61% of finance leaders report a treasury talent shortage.
Only 8% are trying to hire their way out of it.

That gap tells the real story of 2026.

CFOs stopped recruiting harder. The talent does not exist in sufficient supply.

Strategic Treasurer reports treasury roles stay unfilled despite strong employment levels. This is structural, not cyclical.

So the question shifted. Where do scarce treasury hours actually go?

In most enterprise functions, they go to work that requires no judgment:

- Logging into multiple bank portals to pull statements
- Matching transactions against the ERP by hand
- Re-entering payment data that already exists in the source system

When banks and ERP systems are disconnected, someone bridges them manually. Skilled hours get spent on unskilled work.

Hiring does not fix this. A new analyst still inherits the same manual workflow.

This is exactly where direct bank and ERP connectivity becomes critical.

- Reconciliation runs automatically instead of by hand
- Payments initiate from the ERP with no re-entry
- The same team handles more volume without new headcount

The insight is simple. The talent shortage is not only a hiring problem. It is a workflow problem hiding behind one.

How much of your treasury team's week still goes to manual bridging between banks and the ERP?
