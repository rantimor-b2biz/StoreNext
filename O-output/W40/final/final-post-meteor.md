CFOs are raising technology budgets for 2026.

Treasury automation is still stuck near 48%.

The money is moving. The automation rate is not.

According to Gartner's CFO Budget Priorities Survey of 300+ finance leaders, most CFOs plan to increase technology and AI spend for 2026.

Yet Euromoney's 2026 Economic Outlook puts automation across core treasury functions at just 48%. That figure has held for several budget cycles.

So why does funded automation keep stalling?

Because the constraint is not budget. It is data.

Treasury systems sit on top of unconnected sources. Separate bank portals. Different file formats. ERP instances never built to feed treasury directly.

When the connectivity layer is incomplete, even a well-funded project defaults back to manual reconciliation and payment entry.

This is exactly where connectivity becomes critical.

- Bank data arrives automatically, across every banking relationship
- Reconciliation runs against live ERP data
- Cash positioning and forecasting use current numbers, not lagging reports

Gartner also reports 56% of CFOs rank cost optimization and 51% rank forecast accuracy among their top 2026 priorities. Both depend on the same connected data foundation.

Raising the budget line does not raise the automation rate. Closing the connectivity gap does.

Before approving the next automation initiative: does it address bank and ERP connectivity directly, or assume connectivity already exists?
