The supply chain stress index is easing. The people running supply chains disagree.

Maersk surveyed more than 900 companies in November 2025. 78% of supply chain leaders expect disruption to continue for roughly two more years.

Meanwhile the Global Supply Chain Pressure Index fell to -0.16 in November 2025, per EY. On paper, calm.

So why the gap?

Because the risk is not fading. It is changing shape. Government intervention and tariffs are replacing the old shocks. The volatility is now structural, not episodic.

Here is the operating problem.

Most enterprises still manage a two-year condition with two-week tools. Email chains. Spreadsheet trackers. One-off supplier calls, rebuilt from scratch every cycle.

That worked for isolated incidents. It does not scale across hundreds of suppliers and recurring disruption.

This is exactly where a structured supplier collaboration layer becomes critical.

Treated as standing infrastructure, it delivers:

- Self-service supplier status, so teams stop chasing confirmations by phone
- Mass communication to all affected suppliers in one verified update
- A complete audit trail for compliance and board review
- Status that flows into the ERP, not a parallel inbox

The insight is simple. If disruption is permanent, crisis response cannot stay temporary.

CFOs and procurement leaders face a budgeting question, not a tooling one.

Are you funding continuity as standing infrastructure, or still paying for it one crisis at a time?
