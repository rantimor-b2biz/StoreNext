CFOs just named their top liquidity risk for 2026. It is not tariffs. It is not rates.

It is their own cash flow forecasts.

J.P. Morgan's 2026 CFO Outlook survey found 38% of CFOs and treasurers rank forecasting as their biggest liquidity challenge. Market volatility ranks second, at 35%.

That shift matters. The worry has turned inward.

Here is why forecasts keep missing.

It is rarely a skill problem. It is a data problem.

Bank balances sit in one portal. ERP cash positions sit in another system. FX rates get pulled manually, often once a day.

Reconciliation happens after the forecast, not before it.

PwC's 2025 Global Treasury Survey found FX risk is now the top economic exposure for 83% of treasury teams. When exposures are elevated, week-old exports cannot support decisions.

No model can compensate for fragmented inputs.

This is exactly where direct connectivity becomes critical. Not another overlay on broken plumbing.

Connected treasury requires three things:

- Direct bank aggregation, pulled automatically across every relationship
- ERP integration, so cash data shares one source of truth
- Automated FX rates, calculated from live currency data

Forecasting accuracy is an infrastructure outcome, not a forecasting-team outcome.

Before you lock 2026 priorities, ask one question. Does your forward cash view depend on a spreadsheet consolidation step?

If it does, the forecast is built on lagging data by design.
