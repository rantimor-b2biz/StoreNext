For two years, CFOs treated tariffs as a shock to absorb once.

That assumption is now gone.

CFOs say price growth would be roughly 25% lower in 2026 without tariff impact. That is per the Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond and Duke Fuqua CFO Survey.

Tariffs are no longer an event. They are a permanent line item.

Here is why that changes procurement.

Most teams built their processes around annual contract cycles. Renegotiate once. Lock in pricing. Move on.

But tariff fees are now dynamic, per CFO Brew. Pricing shifts trigger contract modifications continuously, across thousands of suppliers.

Email threads and spreadsheets were built for infrequent renegotiation. Not continuous pass-through at volume.

The cost of that gap is real. Grant Thornton's Q1 2026 survey expects 4.5% unit cost growth next year.

Every day between a tariff increase and its documented pass-through is margin leaking out.

This is exactly where centralized supplier communication becomes critical.

- Pricing changes flow through one system of record, not scattered inboxes
- Every supplier sees the same terms, at the same time, with the same audit trail
- Invoice compliance is tracked against updated terms as invoices arrive

The bottleneck is not knowing tariffs are rising. CFOs already know.

The bottleneck is whether procurement can push a change to every supplier and confirm compliance before the next invoice closes.

Awareness does not protect margin. Renegotiation speed does.

If tariffs are a fixed cost in your 2026 plan, how fast can your team pass a pricing change to every affected supplier?
