For the first time in years, cost savings is no longer procurement's top priority.

Supply continuity has overtaken it.

The Hackett Group's 2026 Procurement Agenda study ranks ensuring supply continuity above cost reduction for 2026. Via Ironclad.

This is not a reordered list. It is a redefinition of what procurement is asked to protect.

Here is the gap most organizations are missing.

GEP's 2026 benchmarking report of 311 CPOs names supplier enablement as one of five core performance benchmarks. Alongside savings and spend under management.

But most teams cannot report on it.

Supplier status, documents, and readiness still live in email threads and spreadsheets. Scattered across staff. Impossible to audit at scale.

That is why continuity fails under pressure. Not from lack of intent, but from lack of structure.

You cannot benchmark what you cannot systematically observe.

This is exactly where structured supplier collaboration becomes critical:

- Suppliers update their own status and submit documents, without inbound email volume
- A single auditable record of supplier communication, not a history reconstructed after the fact
- Continuity reported as a metric, connected to the ERP that stays the source of truth

Continuity has to be designed and auditable before the next disruption, not improvised during it.

Can your organization report supplier enablement today, with data instead of anecdotes?
