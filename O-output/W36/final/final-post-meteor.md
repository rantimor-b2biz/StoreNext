63% of finance leaders expect FX volatility to rise in 2026.
89% are not stress-testing their FX forecasts against it.

Most CFOs are treating this as a hedging-strategy problem.

It is not. Not first.

Before treasury can hedge exposure correctly, it has to see that exposure in real time. For most Enterprise treasuries, it still cannot.

Here is why the gap exists.

FX rates and multi-bank balances still move through spreadsheets and periodic ERP updates. An analyst pulls rates once a day, sometimes once a week. Balances across entities get reconciled on a similar cycle.

By the time the data reaches a decision-maker, the rate has moved. The hedge is decided against yesterday's exposure.

Structural volatility does not wait for a weekly batch job.

This is exactly where continuous, ERP-native FX data becomes critical. Not as a replacement for hedging judgment, but as the foundation underneath it:

- FX rates that update continuously inside the ERP, not on a manual schedule
- Multi-bank exposure standardized into one feed across banking partners
- Consolidated position visible the same day it changes, not days later

The ERP stays the source of truth. The data just stays current.

Good hedging decisions still require good judgment. But judgment applied to week-old exposure figures is judgment applied to the wrong picture.

If you are reviewing FX strategy for 2026, which comes first for your team: the hedging program, or the data foundation underneath it?
