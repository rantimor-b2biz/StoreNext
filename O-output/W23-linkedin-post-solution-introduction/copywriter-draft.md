## Copywriter Notes

### Choices Made

- **"The most common answer procurement leaders gave us last week":** Bridges directly from Week 1's CTA ("What's your biggest procurement headache?"), creating narrative continuity across the campaign. Creates the sense of a conversation in progress.
- **The quoted supplier calls:** Concrete, specific, recognizable. Every procurement leader has heard these exact words. This creates immediate identification.
- **"Your procurement team didn't sign up to be a call center":** Reframes the problem at an emotional level. The team is over-qualified for this work. This opens the door to the solution.
- **Solution section uses parallel structure:** "They check... They see... They know... Without calling anyone." Short declarative sentences. Each is a specific, verifiable action, not a vague claim.
- **"20,000+ suppliers already work this way":** This is the critical proof point. It says: this is not theoretical. It is operational, at scale, now.
- **"That's not a feature. That's infrastructure.":** Mirror of the Week 1 closing structure. Reinforces StoreNext's positioning without naming the company. The positioning comes through on repeated exposure.
- **CTA:** Kept the same question from the calendar ("What would your team do with 10 extra hours per week?") - invites positive forward-looking engagement, not just problem venting.

### Alternatives Considered

- Opening with a statistic instead of the "last week" callback. Decided against it: the campaign narrative thread is more valuable than a standalone stat at this stage.
- Including the StoreNext name explicitly in the post. Decided against it: Week 2 is still in the awareness/solution-framing phase, not the conversion phase. The brand name will become more prominent in Weeks 7-13.
- Using a contrast graphic (problem side vs. solution side). Could work as a paired visual. Left as optional; the post stands alone as text.

### Questions for the Gatekeeper

- Does the "last week" callback to Week 1 work if readers didn't see Week 1? (Should it stand alone?)
- Is "call center" the right metaphor, or is "switchboard" (used in Week 1) more consistent?
