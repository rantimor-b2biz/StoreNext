Most S/4HANA migration plans are scoped around the general ledger.

The real risk sits in the bank connections wrapped around it.

SAP mainstream maintenance for ECC ends December 31, 2027 (Technova Partners). A Gartner study cited by Qestit found more than 60% of ECC customers have not begun deep migration work. Timelines are now compressing across the ecosystem.

Here is why bank connectivity is the underestimated part.

When payments and bank feeds live embedded inside the ERP, they inherit its migration timeline and its risk profile. A cutover delay in the ledger becomes a cutover delay in payment execution.

And most treasury data enters migration already fragmented. Only 40% of treasury teams run fully integrated ERP and TMS ecosystems (DXC Technology). Another 40% run partial integrations.

This is exactly where an independent bank connectivity layer becomes critical.

When bank aggregation, payments, and forecasting connect to the ERP rather than sit inside it:

- Treasury keeps real-time cash visibility through every migration stage
- Payment execution does not depend on ERP cutover timing
- Forecasting continues whether the ERP is on ECC, mid-migration, or live on S/4HANA

Treasury advisors including TIS now recommend decoupling bank connectivity from the core S/4HANA scope for this reason.

Bank connectivity and cash visibility are not features of your ERP. They are requirements that should outlast any single system transition.

Where does payment continuity risk sit in your migration plan today: with the ERP team, or with treasury?
