Every CFO owns a bank fee line. Few can fully reconcile it.

In 2026, that gap is getting more expensive.

The Association for Financial Professionals reports a "rubber-band effect." Banks are pushing fees back up after several years of smaller increases.

The timing is the real story. CFO Dive reports a Clearing House executive noting that treasury leaders now demand deeper visibility into payment and fee data as business runs in real time.

Fees are rising. Tolerance for not knowing is falling.

Why is this so hard to see?

Enterprise treasury rarely banks with one institution. Multiple entities, currencies, and jurisdictions mean multiple banking relationships. Each sends its own statement format.

The scale is documented. AFP's benchmark data draws on 85,000+ account analysis statements across 100+ banks worldwide.

A fee labeled one way at one bank appears under a different code, currency, or billing cycle at another. Comparing them means manual extraction and manual mapping.

This is not a competence problem. Treasury teams are working with data never designed to be compared.

This is exactly where direct bank aggregation becomes critical.

- Fee categories comparable across banks, not trapped in each taxonomy
- Fee trends visible over time, not discovered at quarter-end
- Fee increases identifiable within days, not at the next close
- Multi-entity, multi-currency exposure in one view, not a manual spreadsheet

Visibility alone is a compliance outcome. Consolidated fee data is a negotiating position.

An audit trail documents what happened. A negotiating position changes what happens next.

Before your next banking renewal, can you produce one consolidated fee report across every relationship, without manual reconciliation?
