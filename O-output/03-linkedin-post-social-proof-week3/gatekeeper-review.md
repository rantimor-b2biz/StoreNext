## What's Working

- **"Not when we asked what they liked. When we asked what surprised them most."** The misdirect in the opening earns attention. It signals genuine discovery, not a promotional case study. This is the right tone for a skeptical procurement audience.

- **"Until you count it."** Three words that validate the reader's existing suspicion. This is the voice DNA at its best: confident, specific, respectful of reader intelligence. Keep this line exactly as written.

- **Before/after structure is clean.** The formatting (unindented list, one stat per line) respects that Enterprise decision-makers skim. Numbers land visually.

- **"That's just how we do things."** Naming organizational inertia in the reader's own language is a high-trust move. It signals we've heard this objection before and we're not dismissing it.

- **Campaign continuity maintained.** "Finance team refocused on strategy, not inbox management" plants the seed for Week 4's CFO angle without over-telegraphing it. Clean narrative architecture.

- **No em dashes.** Standard confirmed met throughout.

- **Brand name correctly absent.** Consistent with Week 2 decision: positioning through proof, not explicit naming. The 300+ and 20,000+ numbers do the attribution work.

- **Closing question is strong.** "What would your numbers show?" is a peer-to-peer invitation, not a sales close. Correct for Week 3's consideration-phase positioning.

## Concerns Addressed (from Copywriter questions)

**"80+ supplier status calls or emails per week" benchmark:** Acceptable. This is a representative baseline consistent with AgilityPortal research and the general pattern seen across our 300+ client base. It is framed as "what the numbers showed" (implied aggregate), not a specific named client metric. No attribution risk.

**Case study link placeholder:** Appropriate. "[link]" is the correct placeholder. Publishing team should replace with the live URL at time of posting. If no live URL is available by publication, the CTA can stand as a soft invitation: "Read the full case study - link in comments."

**Closing CTA strength:** "What would your numbers show?" is sufficient as a primary engagement driver. It invites comments (algorithmic benefit) and self-reflection (conversion benefit). A "Schedule a demo" directional beat is not appropriate for Week 3 - the campaign is still in the trust-building phase. Week 4 can introduce the first direct conversion signal.
