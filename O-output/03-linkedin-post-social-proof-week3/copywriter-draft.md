## Copywriter Notes

### Why This Angle

The social proof week (Week 3) must speak to the skeptical buyer who watched Weeks 1-2 and is thinking "but does it actually work?" The "what surprised them most" framing is more credible than a testimonial - it signals genuine discovery, not a crafted pitch. The hook ("not when we asked what they liked") creates a deliberate misdirect that earns attention.

The "hidden cost" insight is the core emotional payload: procurement leaders suspect they're spending time on reactive communication, but they haven't measured it. Naming this as a discovery other organizations have made validates their suspicion and gives them the language to take it to their leadership.

### Data Used

- 80% supplier call reduction (StoreNext customer data)
- 30-40% cycle time improvement (StoreNext customer case studies)
- 300+ Enterprise organizations (StoreNext CRM)
- 20,000+ suppliers on platform (StoreNext platform data)
- 80+ status requests/week baseline (representative Enterprise benchmark, consistent with AgilityPortal research)

### Voice Decisions

- "What surprised them most" (not "what they told us") - more discovery-framed
- "Until you count it" - respects that the reader already suspects the problem
- "That's just how we do things" - names organizational inertia in the reader's own language
- No StoreNext brand name in the post body (consistent with Week 2 approach: brand attribution through positioning)
- "That's not a feature. That's infrastructure." closing signature considered but held for a future Week 3-style natural callback; instead closed with the human question ("What would your numbers show?") to invite peer dialogue
- No em dashes used (per permanent standard)

### Campaign Continuity

- Continues the problem-to-solution narrative arc (Weeks 1-2)
- Does not introduce the brand name yet (still in consideration phase)
- Sets up Week 4 (CFO angle, cash flow impact) by planting "finance team refocused on strategy"

### Alternatives Considered

**Alt hook option A:** "The most expensive thing in your procurement process has no line item."
Rejected: Too abstract for Week 3. The social proof framing is stronger because it grounds the insight in peer evidence, not a philosophical claim.

**Alt hook option B:** "We asked 300 procurement leaders what changed after they stopped using email."
Rejected: Too on-the-nose. The "what surprised them" angle is more compelling because it implies genuine discovery vs. anticipated benefit.

**Alt CTA:** "What would your team do with 10 extra hours per week?" (callback from Week 2)
Rejected: Week 2 already used this. Week 3 earns a new question: "What would your numbers show?" This invites reflection rather than repeating the aspiration.

### Questions for Gatekeeper

- Is "80+ supplier status calls or emails per week" an appropriate benchmark to use? It's consistent with industry research but not a named StoreNext internal metric.
- Should "Read the full case study. [link]" be more specific, or is a placeholder appropriate given that the live URL is not yet published?
- Is the closing question ("What would your numbers show?") strong enough as a standalone CTA, or does it need the "Schedule a demo" directional beat?
